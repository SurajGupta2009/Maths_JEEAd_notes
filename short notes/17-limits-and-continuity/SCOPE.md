# Scope — Chapter 17: Limits & Continuity

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Differential Calculus):** limits of real-valued functions, one-sided limits, continuity and its algebra, continuity of composite functions, evaluation of indeterminate forms with L'Hôpital's rule, and the intermediate-value property of continuous functions. Supporting standard limits and asymptotes are included as directly usable limit tools.

Official syllabus PDF: <https://jeeadv.ac.in/documents/jee-advanced-2026-syllabus.pdf>.

## Part I — JEE core and close applications

- Meaning of a finite limit, punctured neighbourhoods, a complete ε–δ proof, one-sided limits, greatest-integer jumps and infinite-limit terminology.
- Limit laws, factoring, geometric-factor identities, radical conjugates and removable-domain distinctions.
- Squeeze theorem, bounded oscillation, the geometric proof of $\sin x/x\to1$, and standard trigonometric limits in radians.
- Exponential and logarithmic first-order limits, $a^x=e^{x\ln a}$, the continuous $e$-limit and logarithmic treatment of $1^\infty$ forms.
- Dominant-power limits at infinity, growth comparisons, horizontal, vertical and oblique asymptotes.
- Indeterminate-form classification and hypothesis-checked use of L'Hôpital's rule, including products, differences and power forms.
- Continuity at a point and on domains, removable/jump/infinite/oscillatory discontinuities, algebra and composition of continuous functions, and piecewise parameter matching.
- Intermediate Value and Bolzano root theorems, bisection, uniqueness as a separate argument, fixed points and mixed absolute-value/fractional-part questions.
- Six visual-method sheets joining exact calculations to open/closed points, squeeze bounds, trigonometric and exponential targets, continuous joins and IVT crossings.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

- Heine's sequential criterion, sequence-based nonexistence, the Dirichlet function and substitution-based positive proofs.
- Stolz–Cesàro, harmonic growth, monotone-and-bounded recursive sequences, Heron's iteration and exponent-tracking recurrences.
- Right-endpoint Riemann-sum limits, power sums, and logarithmic squeeze bounds for products.
- Stirling's formula, central-binomial asymptotics, the Euler–Mascheroni constant, harmonic differences, Big-O remainders and second-order convergence rates.
- IVT constructions for equal values at a half-shift or antipodal pair; continuous additive, Jensen and exponential functional equations.

These are explicitly labelled topic-linked extensions, not a complete IOQM/RMO course. They are not blended into the official core, and no extension is declared impossible in JEE.

## Chapter boundaries

- **Chapter 18:** derivative-from-definition calculations, tangent construction and systematic differentiation remain with Differentiability & Methods. L'Hôpital is retained here because it is a limit-evaluation rule; this chapter does not turn it into a derivatives course.
- **Chapter 21:** Riemann sums appear only as an extension and forward bridge. The definition, theory and full evaluation methods of definite integrals remain with Definite Integration & Areas.
- **Chapters 19–20:** monotonicity is used only where needed to prove uniqueness of an already exhibited root; tangent methods, extrema and antiderivative technique are not taught here.

## Conventions and hypotheses

- A limit concerns $0<|x-a|<\delta$; the value $f(a)$ is irrelevant until continuity is tested. Cancellations are valid on the punctured domain, not as a restoration of an excluded point.
- Trigonometric standard limits use radians and require matching small arguments.
- Real variable exponents require a positive base nearby. The forms $0/0$, $\infty/\infty$, $0\cdot\infty$, $\infty-\infty$, $0^0$, $\infty^0$ and $1^\infty$ must be classified rather than substituted arithmetically.
- L'Hôpital's hypotheses and the remaining indeterminate form must be rechecked after every step.
- A vertical asymptote is not inferred from a cancelled denominator zero; factoring distinguishes holes from poles.
- Continuity requires the value, two-sided limit and their agreement. On a one-sided domain or at an included endpoint, the corresponding one-sided notion is used.
- IVT requires continuity on the whole closed interval and proves existence, not uniqueness. Equal endpoint signs do not prove absence of roots.
- A recursive fixed-point equation is used only after convergence is established. The continuous exponential functional-equation argument excludes the identically zero solution and proves positivity before writing $f(x)=a^x$.
