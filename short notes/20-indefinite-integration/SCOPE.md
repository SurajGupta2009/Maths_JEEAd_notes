# Scope — Chapter 20: Indefinite Integration

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Integral Calculus):** integration as the inverse process of differentiation; indefinite integrals of standard functions; integration by parts; and integration by substitution and partial fractions. This chapter develops exactly that antiderivative toolkit and the domain hypotheses needed to use it correctly.

Official syllabus PDF: <https://jeeadv.ac.in/documents/jee-advanced-2026-syllabus.pdf>.

## Part I — JEE core and close applications

- Antiderivatives on intervals, the meaning of $+C$, initial conditions and the basic power, logarithmic, exponential and trigonometric library.
- Substitution as the reverse chain rule, complete $du$ factors, constant scale corrections and linear inner functions.
- The $g'/g$ logarithmic pattern; tangent, cotangent, secant and cosecant antiderivatives with absolute values and excluded points.
- Algebraic preparation, direct trigonometric-power substitutions, odd-power strategies, half-angle identities and equivalent answers differing by constants.
- Arctangent and arcsine standard forms, coefficient scaling, completing the square, difference-of-squares logarithms and radical standard forms.
- Integration by parts with LIATE, repeated parts, a lone logarithm or inverse function, algebraic products and cyclic exponential-trigonometric products.
- Rational-function division, distinct and repeated linear factors, irreducible quadratics, derivative-plus-remainder numerator splits and the corrected P8.
- The universal $t=\tan(x/2)$ substitution, including the $dx$ conversion and its branch restrictions.
- Six visual-method sheets joining exact calculations to slope diagrams, disconnected logarithm branches, partial-fraction graphs, equivalent antiderivatives and the half-angle branch.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

- Euler substitution for $\sqrt{x^2+a^2}$ and branch-aware radical logarithms, including an initial-value example.
- Reduction formulae for $x^ne^x$, $(\ln x)^n$ and $\sin^nx$, with closed forms checked against recurrence.
- Parameter differentiation of $x^{a+1}/(a+1)$ to generate first and second logarithmic antiderivatives, cross-checked against by parts and reduction.
- Non-elementary antiderivatives and the precise meaning of special functions such as $\mathrm{erf}$ and $\mathrm{Si}$.
- Complexification and undetermined coefficients for $e^{ax}\sin bx$ and $e^{ax}\cos bx$, including damped examples.

These are explicitly labelled topic-linked extensions, not a complete IOQM/RMO course. The underlying by-parts and substitution ideas remain connected to Part I; no extension is declared impossible in JEE.

## Conventions and hypotheses

- An indefinite integral is defined on an interval. Two antiderivatives differ by a constant on that interval; disconnected branches can require independent constants.
- The power rule requires $n\ne-1$. Inverse-trigonometric and difference-of-squares formulas use $a>0$ and retain their stated domain intervals.
- Absolute values in logarithmic antiderivatives do not remove singularities. Algebraic cancellation never restores a point excluded from the original integrand.
- A substitution must account for the full differential. The half-angle substitution is used on one branch, without automatically matching constants across its discontinuities.
- Partial fractions begin with polynomial division when necessary. Repeated factors require every power, and an irreducible quadratic requires a linear numerator.
- Reduction recurrences use consistent antiderivative choices and one final arbitrary constant. Parameter differentiation keeps $x>0$ fixed until after differentiation with respect to $a$.
- “No elementary antiderivative” does not mean “no antiderivative” or “no exact definite value.” Special functions require a defining derivative, normalisation where used, and a domain branch.
