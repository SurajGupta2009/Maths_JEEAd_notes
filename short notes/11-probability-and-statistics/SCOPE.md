# Scope — Chapter 11: Probability & Statistics

Official syllabus includes probability, Bayes' theorem, mean/median/mode, mean
deviation, variance and standard deviation for grouped/ungrouped data, and
mean/variance of a random variable.

## Part I — JEE core / directly applicable models (5 PNG sheets)

- Sample spaces, events, classical probability (only for equally likely
  outcomes), axioms, bounds, complements; addition rule and overlap correction.
- Counting under uniform selection; conditional probability, multiplication
  rule, independence (including exclusive-vs-independent trap), partitions,
  total probability, Bayes and base-rate interpretation.
- Discrete random variables and pmf; expectation, second moment, variance,
  standard deviation, affine changes and indicators; covariance caveat for
  variance sums.
- Binomial model with fixed independent Bernoulli trials, pmf and moments;
  geometric waiting time with support convention and memorylessness. These
  distributions are standard derived models/applications; the syllabus anchor
  is probability and random-variable mean/variance.
- Descriptive statistics: mean, median and mode for raw/frequency data; mean
  absolute deviation about a specified centre; population variance and SD;
  frequency tables; grouped data via class marks; grouped median and mode
  interpolation with assumptions and unequal-class-width warning.

## Part II — Olympiad/specialised extensions (2 PNG sheets)

Continuous geometric probability with coordinate-model caveat; ballot theorem
and reflection; birthday collision; state recurrences, coupon collector,
consecutive-run expectation; gambler's ruin (finite absorbing barriers),
derangements and indicator averaging.

## Conventions and limits

- Classical count ratios require a finite equally-likely sample space.
- Geometric-distribution support counts trials up to and including success;
  a distinct failure-count convention is noted.
- Descriptive variance uses divisor $N$ for the full listed data/population.
  The sample estimator with $N-1$, confidence intervals and hypothesis testing
  are not covered.
- Grouped median/mode and class-mark moments estimate statistics because
  original positions inside classes are unknown; interpolation assumes a
  smooth within-class distribution.
