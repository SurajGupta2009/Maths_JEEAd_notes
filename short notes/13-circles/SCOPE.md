# Scope — Chapter 13: Circles

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Analytical Geometry, 2D):** circle
in various forms; equations of tangent, normal and chord; parametric equations
of a circle; intersection of a circle with a straight line.

## Part I — JEE core and close applications

- Standard/general equation, centre/radius, completion of squares and the
  distinction between a proper real circle, a point-circle and an empty real
  locus.
- Circles through three noncollinear points; circle with a given diameter;
  angular and rational parametrizations (including the omitted rational
  parameter point).
- Secant/tangent/no-intersection classification via centre-to-line distance;
  chord length and solving line–circle intersections.
- Tangent and normal at a point, incidence hypothesis, power, tangent length
  from an external point, chord of contact.
- Radical axis/equal power, common chord, radical centre and two-circle
  position tests as direct applications.

Radical axes, power and two-circle position are useful applications; they are
not presented as separate verbatim bullets in the official circle sentence.
The sheet count is reported in the README after build.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

Normalized coaxal families and radical-centre edge cases; inversion; Simson's
line and Ptolemy's theorem with exact hypotheses and corrected examples. These
are topic-linked Olympiad extensions, not a complete IOQM course.

## Conventions

- A general real circle is normalized to $x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0$; unequal
  coefficients of $x^2,y^2$ or a nonzero $xy$ term describe a different
  quadratic locus in orthogonal axes.
- Chord length formula assumes a real secant or tangent, $d\le r$.
- Tangent-at-point formulas require the point to lie on the circle. Tangent
  lengths and chord of contact are for external points.
- Radical-axis subtraction assumes both circle equations have the same
  coefficient of $x^2+y^2$; concentric unequal circles have no finite
  equal-power line, identical circles have equal power everywhere.
- Circle/line intersections include tangency as a repeated algebraic root.
