# Scope — Chapter 21: Definite Integration & Areas

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Integral Calculus):** definite integrals and their properties, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, and determination of areas bounded by simple curves. This chapter develops those ideas from Riemann accumulation through exact evaluation, convergence and area slicing.

Official syllabus PDF: <https://jeeadv.ac.in/documents/jee-advanced-2026-syllabus.pdf>.

## Part I — JEE core and close applications

- Equal-width Riemann sums, right endpoints, signed accumulation and recognition of a sum as a definite integral.
- FTC Parts 1 and 2, dummy variables, accumulation functions and variable upper/lower limits via the chain rule.
- Definite substitution with transformed endpoints; definite by parts, partial fractions and the branch-aware half-angle substitution.
- Linearity, orientation and additivity; even/odd functions, periodic intervals and King's reflection property.
- Infinite-interval and finite-singularity improper integrals, both $p$-tests, comparison, endpoint logarithmic limits and inversion.
- Absolute convergence, conditional convergence and the distinction between an ordinary improper integral and a Cauchy principal value.
- Signed integral versus total area; vertical top-minus-bottom slices, horizontal right-minus-left strips, splitting at intersections and the ellipse area $\pi ab$.
- Six visual-method sheets joining exact calculations to rectangle, accumulation, reflection, tail and area diagrams.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

- Wallis reduction, even/odd closed forms, the squeeze argument and the infinite Wallis product.
- Beta and Gamma definitions, trigonometric Beta forms, reflection, duplication, Q40's square-root sine/cosine value and the Beta derivation of Wallis's sixth-power result.
- Gaussian squaring in polar coordinates, scaling, completion of the square for Q46, and an additional cosine-transform calculation.
- Frullani's cutoff argument, the canonical exponential examples, and separately justified conditional cosine/sine examples.
- Feynman differentiation for logarithmic powers and Q45, including local domination, anchors and the corrected parameter-integral representation.
- Dirichlet convergence, nonabsolute convergence, the damped parameter family and Abel continuity leading to Q37 $=\pi/2$.
- Additional rational-tail and logarithmic-trigonometric evaluations as clearly labelled enrichment.

These are topic-linked extensions, not a complete IOQM/RMO or real-analysis syllabus. Placement in Part II is organisational; an elementary special case is not declared impossible in JEE.

## Chapter boundaries

- **Chapter 20:** antiderivatives, constants on intervals, indefinite substitution, by parts, partial fractions and half-angle antiderivatives are prerequisites. This chapter uses that toolkit but evaluates definite limits rather than repeating the full indefinite course.
- **Chapter 22:** formation, classification and solution of differential equations are not introduced. A variable-limit accumulation function is not a differential-equation lesson.
- **Special-functions and analysis courses:** Beta/Gamma, conditional convergence and Abel continuity are introduced only to support the stated exact integrals; they are not complete treatments of analytic continuation or convergence theory.

## Conventions and hypotheses

- A definite integral is signed. Total geometric area uses an absolute value or positive top/bottom and right/left differences on each subinterval.
- A definite substitution must transform both endpoints or return to the original variable before evaluation. Reversed limits retain their sign.
- King's property reflects the dummy variable over the same interval; it does not assert pointwise equality and does not remove singularities.
- Every infinite or singular integral is defined by a limit. Convergence is checked before symmetry, inversion, parameter differentiation or algebraic subtraction.
- Absolute convergence concerns $\int|f|$. Conditional convergence does not permit arbitrary rearrangement of positive and negative area. A symmetric principal value need not be an ordinary convergent integral.
- The real integral definitions of Beta and Gamma use positive parameters. Reflection and duplication are applied only with their stated domains.
- Area integrals use intersections and the correct ordering on every subinterval. The plotting window of an improper integral is illustrative, never a convergence proof.
