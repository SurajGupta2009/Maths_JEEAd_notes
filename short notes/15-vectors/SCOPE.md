# Scope — Chapter 15: Vectors

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Vectors):** addition of vectors, scalar multiplication, dot and cross products, scalar triple products and their geometrical interpretations.

Official syllabus PDF: <https://jeeadv.ac.in/documents/jee-advanced-2026-syllabus.pdf>.

## Part I — JEE core and close applications

- Vector language: scalars, free vectors, position vectors and displacements; components in two and three dimensions, magnitude, unit vectors, direction ratios and direction cosines.
- Componentwise addition, scalar multiplication, linear combinations, dependence, the parallelogram law and triangle-inequality equality cases.
- Points and weighted averages: internal and external section formulas, midpoints, triangle and tetrahedron centroids, and the median-concurrency argument.
- Dot products: component and geometric definitions, angles, perpendicularity, sign tests, Cauchy–Schwarz, scalar/vector projection, rejection, unit-vector angle bisectors and work.
- Cross products: component formula, right-hand orientation, anti-commutativity, distributivity, normal directions, parallelogram area and Lagrange's identity.
- Vector geometry: triangle area from two edges, diagonal-area relations, equal-area arguments, the cosine rule and a vector proof of the angle-bisector theorem.
- Scalar triple products: signed volume, determinant permutations, linear dependence, four-point coplanarity, parallelepiped and tetrahedron volumes, and mixed problem selection.
- Six visual-method sheets joining exact calculations to parallelogram symmetry, projection/rejection, area, signed volume, dependence and product selection.

Section formulas, direction cosines, projections, areas and tetrahedron volumes are presented as direct geometric applications of the officially named operations. The sheet count is reported in the README after build.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

- Vector triple products, both BAC–CAB groupings, non-associativity, Jacobi's identity and the scalar quadruple product.
- Normalized and unnormalized barycentric coordinates, area weights, directed-ratio Ceva and Routh's area formula with an exact intersection check.
- Origin choices for vector proofs, Varignon's parallelogram, the circumcentre-origin orthocentre formula and the Euler-line ratio.

These are topic-linked extensions, not a complete IOQM/RMO course. They are separated from the official core rather than presented as additional verbatim JEE syllabus bullets.

## Boundary with Chapter 16

This chapter stops at vector operations and their geometric interpretations. The source module's complete straight-line and plane chapters belong to **Chapter 16 — Three-Dimensional Geometry**. In particular, systematic vector/Cartesian line equations, plane equations, skew-line distance, point-line/point-plane distance formulas, and line/line, line/plane and plane/plane angle theory are not absorbed here.

A normal obtained from a cross product, a coplanarity test, or a base-times-height interpretation appears only where it explains a vector product. It is not a substitute for Chapter 16's full line-and-plane treatment.

## Conventions and hypotheses

- A displacement is always head minus tail. Position vectors depend on the chosen origin; free vectors do not.
- Angles, unit vectors, direction cosines and projections require the relevant direction vectors to be nonzero. The zero vector has no direction or angle.
- Internal section weights are opposite the corresponding segment lengths; external division uses directed ratios or a signed denominator.
- Cross-product order controls orientation. Area and physical volume are nonnegative, while oriented area and signed triple products retain sign information.
- Three edges for four-point coplanarity or tetrahedron volume must start at one common vertex. A zero triple product also covers degenerate configurations and does not promise a unique plane.
- The tetrahedron volume is one-sixth of the corresponding edge parallelepiped volume; the one-third pyramid factor alone is insufficient.
- The circumcentre-origin formula $H=A+B+C$ requires a genuine triangle circumcentre. In 3D, equal distances from a sphere centre are insufficient unless that centre also lies in the triangle's plane.
