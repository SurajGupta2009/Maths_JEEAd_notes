# Scope — Chapter 22: Differential Equations

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Integral Calculus):** formation of differential equations; solution of first-order differential equations with variables separable, homogeneous first-order differential equations, and linear first-order differential equations.

Official syllabus PDF: <https://jeeadv.ac.in/documents/jee-advanced-2026-syllabus.pdf>.

## Part I — JEE core and canonical close applications

- Order, degree, general and particular solutions, IVPs, verification by substitution, solution intervals and formation by eliminating arbitrary constants.
- Variables separable, including branch selection and constant solutions that can be lost during division.
- Homogeneous first-order equations through $y=vx$, with logarithmic, radicand and blow-up domains stated explicitly.
- Linear first-order equations and integrating factors; Bernoulli substitutions; exponential, polynomial and trigonometric forcing.
- Exact differential forms, potentials, orthogonal trajectories and finite-versus-vertical slope audits.
- Constant-coefficient second-order equations, repeated and complex roots, and Cauchy–Euler trial powers on $x>0$.
- Continuous growth, Newton cooling, autonomous reduction through $p(y)$, and the three damping regimes.
- Six visual-method sheets joining slope fields and exact formulas to IVP branches, orthogonality, damping, non-uniqueness and envelopes.

The official anchor is the first-order material. The canonical paper also labels several second-order, exact, orthogonal, modelling and damping questions as JEE Main or JEE Advanced practice; they remain in Part I as close applications of the same calculus toolkit. This organisational choice is not a claim that every such subtype is explicitly named in the official 2026 bullet.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

- Reduction of order from a known solution, Wronskian independence and the corrected Q34 sign.
- Clairaut equations, general straight-line families and singular envelopes.
- Local existence and uniqueness hypotheses, non-Lipschitz examples and delayed departures.
- Lagrange's equation, the corrected Q38 parametric differentiation and constant-slope branches.
- Riccati equations from a known particular solution, omitted equilibria and denominator-zero intervals.
- Additional enrichment on nonhomogeneous second-order linear equations: undetermined coefficients, resonance and variation of parameters.

These are topic-linked extensions, not a complete IOQM/RMO, dynamical-systems or real-analysis syllabus. Placement in Part II is organisational; an elementary special case is not declared impossible in JEE.

## Chapter boundaries

- **Chapter 18:** derivative rules, implicit differentiation and the chain rule are prerequisites, especially for formation and exact equations.
- **Chapters 20–21:** antiderivatives, partial fractions, logarithms and definite evaluation are used throughout; this chapter changes the unknown from an integral to a function satisfying a differential relation.
- **Further analysis courses:** Picard iteration, stability theory, linear systems, Sturm–Liouville theory and PDEs are not developed. The existence discussion is limited to the stated local hypotheses and explicit counterexamples.

## Conventions and hypotheses

- A solution is always tied to an interval. A formula is not continued through a pole, a zero denominator, a forbidden logarithm or a negative real square-root input.
- Before dividing by $y$, $p$, $v$, $x$ or another expression, constant and equilibrium branches are checked separately. Finite arbitrary constants do not automatically include every singular or equilibrium solution.
- For homogeneous first-order equations, “homogeneous” means dependence on $y/x$; for linear equations it means zero forcing. The two uses are distinguished.
- Orthogonal slopes use $-1/p$ only for finite nonzero $p$. Vertical and horizontal directions are restored geometrically.
- Cauchy–Euler formulas are used on an interval not containing $x=0$; initial points with $x>0$ select the positive branch.
- Continuous relative growth and discrete annual compounding are different models. Newton cooling acts on temperature excess over the environment.
- A damped sinusoid is not periodic. For Q29, consecutive zeros are $\pi/2$ apart, the oscillation/quasi-period is $\pi$, and shifting by $\pi$ multiplies values by $e^{-\pi}$.
- Numerical RK4 agreement supplements, but never replaces, the printed exact derivations and branch audits.
