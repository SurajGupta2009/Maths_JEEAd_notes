# Scope — Chapter 16: Three-Dimensional Geometry

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Three-Dimensional Geometry):** direction cosines and direction ratios; Cartesian and vector equations of a line; coplanar and skew lines; shortest distance between two lines; Cartesian and vector equations of a plane; angle between two lines, two planes, and a line and a plane; and distance of a point from a plane.

Official syllabus PDF: <https://jeeadv.ac.in/documents/jee-advanced-2026-syllabus.pdf>.

## Part I — JEE core and close applications

- Coordinates in space, displacements, distance, direction ratios, direction cosines, normalization and signed projection onto a direction.
- Vector, parametric, two-point, symmetric and general-form equations of lines; zero-denominator conventions and the intersection direction of two independent planes.
- Angles between lines, parallel/coincident classification, nonparallel coplanarity, intersection by parameters and skew-line classification.
- Perpendicular feet and images in a line, point-line distance by projection or cross-product height, and the minimizing-parameter interpretation.
- Shortest distance between nonparallel skew lines as volume divided by base area, exact common-perpendicular feet, and the separate parallel-line method.
- Plane equations from a point and normal, three noncollinear points, intercepts, two edges and a normal cross product.
- Acute angles between two planes and between a line and a plane, including the line-normal complement and intersection directions.
- Point-plane distance, parallel-plane distance, perpendicular feet and reflections in a plane.
- Plane pencils, the complete homogeneous pencil, normalized equal-distance equations and the two bisector planes.
- Six visual-method sheets joining exact calculations to displacement, two-plane intersection, line reflection, skew separation, plane reflection and formula selection.

Coordinates, projections, feet/images, parallel-plane distance, pencils and bisector planes are included as derivable supporting tools for the official line/plane results. They are not presented as extra verbatim syllabus bullets. The sheet count is reported in the README after build.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

- Sphere equations, completing squares, diameter form and the unique sphere through four noncoplanar points.
- Plane sections of spheres, section centres and radii, tangent planes and the centre-distance tangency test.
- Power, radical planes and axes, orthogonal spheres, polar planes, reciprocal polars and the tangent cone/contact circle.
- Tetrahedron inradius and incentre, the volume/surface-area relation, circumcentres and concurrence of the six Monge midplanes.
- Spherical triangles and excess, spherical octants, regular-tetrahedron inradius/circumradius/volume, dihedral angles and solid angle.

These are clearly labelled topic-linked extensions, not a complete IOQM/RMO course. Sphere and advanced geometry are kept outside Part I rather than being blended into the official core.

## Conventions and hypotheses

- Direction cosines and angles require nonzero directions. Direction ratios become direction cosines only after normalization.
- Symmetric line equations with zero denominators must be rewritten parametrically or with fixed coordinate equations.
- Parallel lines need a separate coincident-versus-distinct test. The nonparallel coplanarity test uses a scalar triple product; nonzero means skew.
- The skew-distance formula requires a nonzero cross product of directions. A zero numerator with nonzero denominator instead gives coplanar intersecting lines.
- Three points determine a plane only when they are noncollinear; two plane normals determine an intersection direction only when their cross product is nonzero.
- Line-plane angle uses sine because it is measured from the plane. Line-line and plane-plane acute angles use normalized absolute cosines.
- Distance formulas normalize residuals and preserve the absolute value. Parallel-plane constants must be compared only after matching normal coefficients.
- The finite pencil $P_1+\lambda P_2=0$ omits $P_2=0$; the complete homogeneous form is $\alpha P_1+\beta P_2=0$ with $(\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)$.
- A sphere's centre-distance must be compared with its radius before calling a plane section a circle or a tangent plane.
- Spherical-triangle angles are dihedral angles between great-circle planes; face angles of a regular tetrahedron are the spherical triangle's sides, not its angles.
