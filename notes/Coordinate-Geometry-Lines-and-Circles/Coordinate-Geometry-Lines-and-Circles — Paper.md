## A · Coordinates, distance & locus (Q1–Q4)

#### **Q1**[JEE Main][distance]Find the distance between $(1,2)$ and $(4,6)$.

**Answer:** $5$

#### **Q2**[JEE Main][midpoint]Find the midpoint of the join of $(1,2)$ and $(5,8)$.

**Answer:** $(3,5)$

#### **Q3**[JEE Main][area]Find the area of the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(3,0)$, $(0,4)$.

**Answer:** $6$

#### **Q4**[JEE Adv][locus]Find the locus of points equidistant from $(0,0)$ and $(4,0)$.

**Answer:** $x=2$

## B · The straight line (Q5–Q10)

#### **Q5**[JEE Main][line]Find the line through $(2,3)$ with slope $4$.

**Answer:** $4x-y-5=0$

#### **Q6**[JEE Main][angle]Find the angle between $y=2x+1$ and $y=-\dfrac{x}{3}+2$.

**Answer:** $\tan^{-1}7\approx81.87^\circ$

#### **Q7**[JEE Main][distance]Find the distance from $(1,2)$ to $3x+4y-5=0$.

**Answer:** $\dfrac65=1.2$

#### **Q8**[JEE Adv][foot]Find the foot of the perpendicular from $(1,2)$ to $x+y=0$.

**Answer:** $\left(-\dfrac12,\dfrac12\right)$

#### **Q9**[JEE Adv][perpendicular]Show that $3x+4y+5=0$ and $4x-3y+7=0$ are perpendicular.

**Answer:** yes — slopes $-\dfrac34$ and $\dfrac43$ multiply to $-1$

#### **Q10**[JEE Main][intercept]Find the line whose intercepts on the axes are $3$ and $4$.

**Answer:** $4x+3y=12$

## C · Pairs of straight lines (Q11–Q14)

#### **Q11**[JEE Main][pair]Find the two lines represented by $2x^2+3xy-2y^2=0$.

**Answer:** $y=2x$ and $x+2y=0$

#### **Q12**[JEE Adv][angle]Find the angle between the lines $x^2-2y^2=0$.

**Answer:** $\tan^{-1}(2\sqrt2)\approx70.53^\circ$

#### **Q13**[JEE Adv][bisector]Find the bisectors of the angles between $3x+4y-5=0$ and $4x-3y+7=0$.

**Answer:** $x-7y+12=0$ and $7x+y+2=0$

#### **Q14**[JEE Adv][condition]State the condition for $ax^2+2hxy+by^2=0$ to represent a pair of perpendicular lines.

**Answer:** $a+b=0$

## D · The circle (Q15–Q19)

#### **Q15**[JEE Main][circle]Find the centre and radius of $x^2+y^2-2x-4y-4=0$.

**Answer:** centre $(1,2)$, radius $3$

#### **Q16**[JEE Main][circle]Find the circle through $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$.

**Answer:** $x^2+y^2-x-y=0$

#### **Q17**[JEE Main][circle]Write the circle with centre $(2,-3)$ and radius $5$.

**Answer:** $x^2+y^2-4x+6y-12=0$

#### **Q18**[JEE Adv][circle]Does $x^2+y^2+2x+2y+5=0$ represent a real circle? Justify.

**Answer:** no — $g^2+f^2-c=1+1-5=-3<0$

#### **Q19**[JEE Adv][diameter]Find the circle on the join of $(1,2)$ and $(5,8)$ as diameter.

**Answer:** $(x-3)^2+(y-5)^2=13$

## E · Tangents, chords & power (Q20–Q25)

#### **Q20**[JEE Main][tangent]Find the tangent to $x^2+y^2=25$ at $(3,4)$.

**Answer:** $3x+4y=25$

#### **Q21**[JEE Main][normal]Find the normal to $x^2+y^2=25$ at $(3,4)$.

**Answer:** $4x-3y=0$

#### **Q22**[JEE Main][power]Find the length of the tangent from $(5,5)$ to $x^2+y^2=9$.

**Answer:** $\sqrt{41}\approx6.4031$

#### **Q23**[JEE Adv][chord]Find the length of the chord cut off by $x+y=5$ on $x^2+y^2=25$.

**Answer:** $5\sqrt2\approx7.0711$

#### **Q24**[JEE Adv][chord of contact]Find the tangent length from $(5,0)$ to $x^2+y^2=9$ and the points of contact.

**Answer:** length $4$; contacts $\left(\dfrac95,\pm\dfrac{12}{5}\right)$

#### **Q25**[JEE Adv][radical]Find the radical axis of $x^2+y^2=9$ and $x^2+y^2-4x+6y-3=0$.

**Answer:** $2x-3y-3=0$

## F · Position & coaxal systems (Q26–Q29)

#### **Q26**[JEE Adv][position]Do the circles $x^2+y^2=4$ and $(x-3)^2+y^2=4$ intersect? Give the intersection points if so.

**Answer:** yes — $\left(\dfrac32,\pm\dfrac{\sqrt7}{2}\right)$

#### **Q27**[JEE Adv][radical]Find the radical axis of $x^2+y^2=4$ and $(x-3)^2+y^2=4$.

**Answer:** $x=\dfrac32$

#### **Q28**[Olympiad][coaxal]Find the coaxal system of circles through $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$.

**Answer:** $x^2+y^2-2+\lambda(x-y)=0$

#### **Q29**[Olympiad][inversion]Invert the point $(3,4)$ in the unit circle.

**Answer:** $\left(\dfrac3{25},\dfrac4{25}\right)$

## G · Apollonius & distance problems (Q30–Q34)

#### **Q30**[Olympiad][distance]Find the shortest distance from $(1,2)$ to the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=9$.

**Answer:** $3-\sqrt5\approx0.7639$

#### **Q31**[Olympiad][Apollonius]For $A=(0,0)$ and $B=(4,0)$, find the locus of $P$ with $\dfrac{PA}{PB}=2$.

**Answer:** $\left(x-\dfrac{16}{3}\right)^2+y^2=\dfrac{64}{9}$

#### **Q32**[JEE Adv][locus]Find the locus of points whose distance from the origin is twice their distance from $(3,0)$.

**Answer:** $(x-4)^2+y^2=4$

#### **Q33**[JEE Adv][coaxal]Find the coaxal system of circles through $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$.

**Answer:** $x^{2}+y^{2}-2+\lambda(x-y)=0$, with radical axis $x-y=0$

#### **Q34**[JEE Adv][inversion]Invert $(3,4)$ in the unit circle.

**Answer:** $\left(\dfrac3{25},\dfrac4{25}\right)=(0.12,0.16)$

## H · Olympiad frontier (Q35–Q46)

#### **Q35**[Olympiad][radical centre]Find the radical centre of $x^2+y^2=1$, $x^2+y^2-4x=0$ and $(x-1)^2+(y-1)^2=1$.

**Answer:** $\left(\dfrac14,\dfrac34\right)$

#### **Q36**[Olympiad][inversion]Under inversion in the unit circle, what curve does the line $x=2$ become?

**Answer:** the circle $X^2+Y^2=\dfrac{X}{2}$ (through the origin, centre $(\frac14,0)$, radius $\frac14$)

#### **Q37**[Olympiad][nine-point]For $A(0,0)$, $B(4,0)$, $C(1,3)$, find the centre and radius of the nine-point circle.

**Answer:** centre $\big(\frac32,1\big)$, radius $\dfrac{\sqrt5}{2}\approx1.118034$

#### **Q38**[Olympiad][nine-point]A triangle has circumradius $5$. What is the radius of its nine-point circle?

**Answer:** $\dfrac52$

#### **Q39**[Olympiad][Euler line]In what order and ratio do $O$, $G$, $N$, $H$ lie on the Euler line, where $N$ is the nine-point centre?

**Answer:** in the order $O,G,N,H$ with $OG:GN:NH=2:1:3$ (equivalently $OG:GH=1:2$ and $N$ bisects $OH$)

#### **Q40**[Olympiad][Simson]For $A(0,0)$, $B(4,0)$, $C(1,3)$ and $P\approx(3.8455,2.2626)$ on the circumcircle, verify that the three perpendicular feet are collinear.

**Answer:** the feet are $\approx(3.8455,0)$, $(2.7915,1.2085)$, $(1.0633,3.1900)$; their $2\times2$ determinant is $\approx-4.4\times10^{-16}$, i.e. $0$ ✓

#### **Q41**[Olympiad][Simson]State the Simson line theorem and the exact condition on $P$.

**Answer:** the feet of the perpendiculars from $P$ to the sides of $ABC$ are collinear **iff** $P$ lies on the circumcircle of $ABC$

#### **Q42**[Olympiad][Ptolemy]For $A(0,0)$, $B(4,0)$, $C(1,3)$ and $D\approx(3.5579,-0.6041)$, all on the circle centred $(2,1)$ with radius $\sqrt5$, verify Ptolemy's theorem.

**Answer:** in circular order $A,D,B,C$: $AB\cdot DC=17.678007=AD\cdot BC+DB\cdot CA$ ✓

#### **Q43**[Olympiad][Ptolemy]A cyclic quadrilateral has sides $3,4,5,6$ in order and diagonals $d_1,d_2$. If $d_1=7$, find $d_2$.

**Answer:** $d_1d_2=3\cdot5+4\cdot6=39$, so $d_2=\dfrac{39}{7}\approx5.571$

#### **Q44**[Olympiad][Ptolemy]Show that Pythagoras' theorem is a special case of Ptolemy's.

**Answer:** take a cyclic quadrilateral with one diagonal a diameter; the two angles it subtends are right angles, and Ptolemy reduces to $c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}$

#### **Q45**[Olympiad][position]Do the circles $x^{2}+y^{2}=4$ and $(x-3)^{2}+y^{2}=4$ intersect?

**Answer:** yes — the centre distance is $3$, between $r_1+r_2=4$ and $\lvert r_1-r_2\rvert=0$

#### **Q46**[Olympiad][Apollonius]Find the circle of Apollonius for $A=(0,0)$, $B=(6,0)$ and $\dfrac{PA}{PB}=\frac12$.

**Answer:** $(x-6)^{2}+y^{2}=4(x^{2}+y^{2})$, i.e. $\left(x+2\right)^2+y^{2}=16$

> [!note] Exam technique notes
> - Convert the geometry to algebra immediately: name the point $(x,y)$, write
>   the condition, simplify.
> - For tangents use the $T=0$ replacement; for normals remember the normal
>   passes through the **centre**.
> - Subtract two circle equations to get a radical axis — it is always a line.
> - Before claiming two circles meet, compare the centre distance with
>   $r_1+r_2$ and $\lvert r_1-r_2\rvert$.
> - In any problem about a triangle, compute $O$ and $H$ first: $N=\frac{O+H}{2}$
>   then hands you the nine-point circle, and $G=\frac{A+B+C}{3}$ the centroid.
> - Before quoting Ptolemy, put the four vertices in **circular order** — the
>   statement is not symmetric under arbitrary relabelling.
