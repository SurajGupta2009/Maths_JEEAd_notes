# Scope — Chapter 12: Straight Lines & Locus

**Official JEE Advanced 2026 anchor (Analytical Geometry, 2D):** Cartesian
coordinates, distance and section formulae, shift of origin; straight lines in
various forms, angle between two lines, point–line distance, lines through the
intersection of two lines, angle bisectors, concurrency; centroid,
orthocentre, incentre and circumcentre of a triangle.

## Part I — JEE core and close applications

- Coordinate displacement, distance, midpoint, internal/external section,
  origin translation, determinant area/collinearity and centroid formula.
- Line forms and their hypotheses; slope including vertical/horizontal
  special cases, normal form, angle, parallel/perpendicular tests, distance
  and foot of perpendicular.
- Pencil through an intersection, finite concurrency versus parallel
  degeneracy, normalized angle bisectors.
- Four official triangle centres: centroid, circumcentre, orthocentre,
  incentre; definitions, construction equations and coordinate checks.
- Locus method: equidistance, fixed distance, distance ratio (perpendicular
  bisector when ratio is 1; Apollonius circle otherwise), with domain and
  squaring checks.
- Homogeneous pair of lines as a useful derived technique. It is explicitly
  distinguished from a separately named official syllabus bullet.

Area/collinearity, Apollonius loci and homogeneous pairs are useful coordinate
applications rather than additional quoted syllabus bullets. Sheet count is
reported in the README after build.

## Part II — Olympiad / specialised extensions

Euler-line ratios, a coordinate proof pattern for the nine-point circle, and
further locus reasoning. These are relevant geometry extensions, not a claim
of complete IOQM coverage.

## Conventions

- $AP:PB=m:n$ is an unsigned distance ratio; internal and external formulas
  are stated separately. External division requires $m\ne n$.
- Lines use $ax+by+c=0$ with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$; direction and normal vectors
  prevent the vertical-line slope exception from breaking tests.
- The concurrency determinant vanishing allows a common point at infinity
  (parallel lines); a finite concurrency claim checks nonparallel directions.
- Triangle-centre formulas assume a nondegenerate triangle. For an equilateral
  triangle, $O=G=H=I$ and the Euler-line direction is not unique.
- Vectors are written as coordinate pairs relative to a common origin.
